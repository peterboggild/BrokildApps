Brokild VR

Rooms and machines that run in the headset's own browser

The instruments are things you listen to. These are things you sit inside. They are WebXR pages: you open a link in the Quest browser, press Start, and you are in the room — no store, no sideloading, no account, no download.

Everything is made at runtime. The geometry is built in code, the planets and surfaces are procedural textures generated when the page loads, and every sound is synthesised in the Web Audio API rather than played back from a file. That is why a whole world is one HTML file of about 60 kB and why there is nothing to wait for.

Each page also runs flat — on a laptop with the mouse and keyboard, or on a phone with on-screen controls — so you can look at one without putting a headset on, and hand the link to someone who doesn't own one.

Conceived and specified by me and programmed with the help of artificial intelligence coding software, like the rest of this site.

The rooms

Portalkniven

Live WebXR Quest 3 Passthrough Room scan Story Flat fallback Dansk

A knife that cuts doors in the air. Hold the trigger, draw a ring in your own living room, close it, and a green crackling oval lights up: an open door to another version of the same room. It is a real window, not a picture: each eye looks through it from its own place, and you walk through. On the other side the Quest's room scan has turned your walls and furniture into mossy ground, riveted steel or layered sandstone, so the other world fits what you can actually walk in.

There is a story (in Danish): a letter from yourself in another dimension, the knife, nine parts of a time machine hidden in three worlds, the machine built by hand on your own table, a trip to the past to make the knife you found, and home again. After that, or in free play, the door also leads to deep space (fly among the stars, bend the sky with a warp and a black hole), to six endless fractal dimensions you fly through and reshape, and to a furnished four-dimensional room that you pull and turn through our three dimensions: a table with eight legs, a ring that comes apart into two balls. You have gloved hands that follow the controllers; stalks, tall grass, hanging hooks and a valve wheel give when you push them; and each of the three story worlds has its own track by the band Tidejord.

Ræs i Bordbyen

Live WebXR Quest 3 Passthrough Room scan Racing Flat fallback Dansk

Build a little town on your real table, then shrink down into it and race. Draw roads, buildings, hills and even tunnels with ramps down to them. The headset finds the table in its room scan; a palette on your left hand holds roads, houses, town blocks, towers, a town hall, a church, parks and squares, and your right hand draws them: sweep to lay a road, drag to place a block. Put a start/finish and a few gates on the roads.

Pick one of four chunky toy cars from the table's edge, and the town grows up round you until it is full size and the room gives way to sky and fields. Three laps through the gates in order, against three opponents who find their own way through your streets; take shortcuts if you dare. Drive from the seat or from behind, with a stick or by holding a wheel in the air, then grow back up to the table and build on.

Guldplaneten

Live WebXR Quest 3 Flat fallback Walk outside Danish

You start outside on the apron at a Danish spaceport, beside a 21-metre heavy lander you can walk underneath. Hop to the ladder, climb aboard, and run the start sequence — five switches and levers that light up in order — then the big red button. Fly the U-shaped yoke up through the atmosphere until the stars come out, pick Månen, Mars or Guldplaneten, jump through hyperspace, and fly down onto endless procedural mountains — then open the hatch and walk around outside, among the alien houses if you picked the gold one. Three flight modes, from an autopilot that flies and lands itself to full three-axis control. The ship cannot be broken. Every planet surface is an escape-time fractal, generated in the browser at load.

Mutant Rotte Angreb

Live WebXR Quest 3 Passthrough Room scan Flat fallback Dansk

The rats in your house have evolved and want the house for themselves. They roll into your own room in little tanks with a rat in the hatch (two in the black ones), hide behind your real furniture — the Quest's room scan cuts them off, so only the part round the corner shows — and shoot at your headset, but only with a clear line of sight. From round 3 kamikaze rats with fireworks on their backs run for your feet, beeping like a bomb, and go off with a bang; a rat trap in your left hand (a mine you lob and leave, four at a time) catches them. From round 6, about every other round, a cheese bomb turns up: pocket it, and when you are about to be overrun throw it (A or X); while its fuse burns every tank, helicopter and rat nearby forgets you and goes for the cheese, and then it goes off. From round 8 giant green super rats glow and shed goo. A rocket launcher in each hand that only nudges its rocket, so you have to aim; rockets run out (grab ammo crates from the floor, or hold the trigger for the machine gun) and fewer rockets means more points. Black armoured tanks arrive from round 6, helicopters from round 8. The whole game is in Danish and checks that your room has been scanned. Health runs out, game over.

Lysrytme

Test WebXR Quest 3 Sound Flat fallback Dansk

Light that flickers at a frequency you choose, from 2 to 40 times a second, like the light glasses meant to set the pace of brain waves — but filling the whole view of a Quest 3, with patterns and colours: rings, a spiral, a reversing checkerboard, colour gradients, the two eyes in turn, a calm centre with only the edges flickering. Five waveforms from a hard blink to a slow swell. The display runs at 120 Hz, so the page tells you which frequencies are exact (40, 30, 10, 6 Hz …) and which will be slightly uneven. Ready-made sessions glide from alpha to theta for meditation, or down to delta for sleep, with Sleeper Agent's noise and ambient themes underneath and a tone that pulses in exactly the light's rhythm. An experiment, not a treatment, and not for anyone sensitive to flashing light.

Planet Designer

Test WebXR Quest 3 PC Danish / English

Design a star and a solar system of your own, and build a planet from its iron core to its oceans and air. The app works out, all the time, whether the world could host life, and whether a human could walk on it without a suit, and shows why in a ledger of reasons with their sources. Drag planets on the orrery, find the green slot where a second habitable world would orbit stably, and fly down to a procedurally detailed planet whose sea level follows from how much water you gave it; cut it open to see its layers. This is a working version that grows step by step: it has a seasonal climate model, a scattering atmosphere, clouds, an explanation card for every verdict and a map of causes. In the Quest 3 press Enter VR to step into the workshop: turn the star's dials at the forge and watch its colour flood the platform, drag planets on the orrery table, and walk round the planet in its cradle. Left stick flies, right stick turns, the grips grab the world, Y or B opens the panel.

Fractal Video

Test WebXR Quest 3 Microphone Flat editor

The music-video fractal machine, opened in the headset: you stand inside a ray-marched 3D fractal while it plays its arrangement of scenes. It listens to the room through the headset's own microphone, so any music playing nearby moves it; no audio file is needed. In the Audio panel press Listen with microphone and allow it, then go to the Render tab, Live VR, and press Enter VR. Inside, the left stick flies and the right stick turns. Hold both triggers and pull your hands apart to make the fractal bigger. Hold a grip and the fractal twists with your hand. Y opens a quick panel of fractals and looks, and A plays and pauses. Point at the fractal and pull the trigger for a palette of sound links: bass, beats, highs, mids, level, and how wild they are. When the frame rate drops, the picture drops in sharpness at once, and if that is not enough you are offered a faster fractal. If it is still too slow, open the same page in Chrome on a PC with the Quest on a cable (Link) and let the PC's graphics card do the work. The sound is analysed on the headset and never leaves it.

Illusion Studio

Test WebXR Quest 3 Hand tracking Passthrough Flat editor

Build 3D scenes whose every surface is covered in a self-moving optical illusion: honeycomb tiles with a light edge on one side and a dark edge on the other, Kitaoka's rotating snakes, Fraser–Wilcox gradients, Pinna–Brelstaff squares, Ouchi, Enigma, the scintillating grid and the café wall. Nothing moves, yet floors flow into holes and balls seem to roll. Spaces (a floor with a funnel-shaped hole, a tunnel, a room, a dome) hold objects (spheres, tori, boxes, cylinders, cones), and bumps, ripples, waves, twists and bulges bend them all, the pattern stretching with the surface. On the desktop you compose the scene and export a JPG of up to 3000 pixels in square, print, social-media or banner formats.

Press Enter VR to stand inside it. Y, B or the button on your left wrist opens a panel with six tabs (Scene, Object, Pattern, Colours, Distort, View); use it with the trigger, or poke it with a finger if you use hand tracking. Point at an object and hold the trigger to carry it. Grab empty space to move the world; grab with two hands and pull them apart or together to scale it, from a dollhouse on a table, through life size, to being as small as an ant among giant balls. The View tab has the same three sizes as buttons. The left stick flies and the right stick turns. Enter mixed reality puts the objects in your own room.

2DPHYS Atomic Workbench

Live WebXR Quest 3 Flat fallback See-through Molecular dynamics Dansk / English

A lab bench where the atoms are real simulations. Graphene, hBN, MoS2, nanotubes and molecules sit on the table, run by proper interatomic potentials, and you work on them with your hands: pull an atom out of a sheet until the bond snaps, put it back and watch it bind, lay one flake on top of another, heat and cool the bench with the thermometer, and send current through a ribbon to see where the electrons go. Three levels — Kid, Adult and Researcher — decide how many tools are on the toolbar, and a camera on the desk saves photos of what you built. Press Real table instead of Enter VR and the lab stands on the table in your own room, seen through the headset. A nearly invisible glass dome, the glovebox, keeps the atoms on the table: they bounce off it and calm down, while your hands reach straight through; a button on the table switches it on and off. Each hand can hold its own tool, so one hand can keep a flake while the other adds atoms to it. Pause stops every atom; while paused you can pick crystals up and arrange them, and Run warms them back up over five seconds, all the way from 1 K. Made for the 2DPHYS group at DTU, where it is also published.

Journey to the Moon

Test Quest 3 In development

The start of a realistic Apollo-style flight to the Taurus-Littrow valley, where Apollo 17 landed. So far only the Moon itself, to be judged before the spacecraft are built: three views of one Moon made from NASA's Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter data — from lunar orbit at 110 km, from a low pass at 15 km, and standing on the valley floor at the landing site. The Sun stands where it stood at the landing, 13° above the eastern horizon, so every crater and rock throws a long shadow. The lander is a stand-in. New: chapter 1, the launch — the countdown, liftoff and the climb to orbit from the Commander's couch of the Command Module, with the escape tower blowing away to uncover the windows three minutes up. New: chapter 2, to the Moon — Earth orbit, the burn that leaves it, turning round to dock with the lunar module and pull it free, and three days of coasting with the time warp, until the Moon's gravity takes over. New: chapter 3, lunar orbit — the burn into orbit behind the Moon with the radio silent, Earthrise in the window beside you, and a pass at 15 km over the landing site, on the same Moon as the test views. New: chapter 4, into the LM — pull yourself hand over hand through the docking tunnel, stand at the Commander's station in the Lunar Module, and watch the Command Module undock and come round to look at your legs. New: chapter 5, the descent — standing at the Commander's station: braking with the windows to the sky, the pitch-over with the valley swinging into view, and the last 150 metres by the computer or by your own hand, dust and all, down to where you then walk.

Opening one in a headset
1 · Open the browser

In the Quest, open the Meta Quest Browser from the app bar. These pages are ordinary web pages; nothing is installed and nothing appears in your library.

2 · Go to the page

Type the address, or send yourself the link and open it from there. It has to be the page's own tab — WebXR is usually blocked inside an embedded frame.

3 · Sit down, then press Start

Entering VR needs a button press, which is why there is one. Sit down first: the seat calibrates to your eye height the moment the session opens.

4 · If something is off

Both grips at once re-centres the seat; the left thumbstick raises and lowers it. If the Start button is greyed out, the page is in a frame or the browser has no XR device.

Why these exist

A headset is mostly used as a console: you buy a thing, install it, and it runs. But the browser inside it is a full WebXR runtime, and a single HTML file served from a static host can put you in a room with physical controls that you reach out and press. That is a strange and underused fact, and these pages exist to use it.

Free, no installer, no account, no telemetry, nothing uploaded. The source for each one is a single readable file in the repo, and you are welcome to take it apart and make it yours.
