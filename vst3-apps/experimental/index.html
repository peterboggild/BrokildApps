The Experimental Collection

Instruments that exist to ask a question about how sound can be made. Each one is a thesis rather than a product.

Each of these exists to ask a question about how sound can be made. A synth played by photographs, a synth that reads a scanned volume, a ball rolling on terrain, surgery on a singing voice, one voice becoming four. They are not products, and the source for every one of them is in the same repository as this page, for anyone who wants to take one further.

Every plug-in in here is byte-for-byte the same file as its own download. The builder re-extracts the finished archive and hashes each one against its individual zip before publishing, and checks that nothing else is in there.

The instruments

Brain Scan

A synth that stores no waveforms but a volume — a scalar field filling a cube, the kind of number a scanner records — and reads a curve through it.

High Tide

A wavetable synth that stores no waveforms either: every frame is a bowl on a sculpted terrain, and the sound is what a mass does when it is dropped into it, integrated by Newton's law at audio rate.

Photo Synth

A synthesiser you play by moving a cursor across a photograph.

The effects

Hairfryer

A free Windows VST3 vocal strip that attempts to convert a clean, loud singing voice into a metal-ish one — growls, gutturals, pig squeals, fry based on physics.

Legion

One voice in, a choir of it out.

What you are getting, honestly

Some of these have a standalone and a handbook; some have a bundle and a README. That is the point of this shelf rather than an oversight: holding an experiment back until it has every accessory means never shipping it at all. Each folder carries whatever that plug-in actually has.

Copy each .vst3 folder — the whole folder, not the file inside it — into C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\Experimental\ and rescan in your DAW. A sub-folder is fine; hosts look inside.

They are AGPLv3, like everything else here. Read the source, change it, build it, and publish your changes if you distribute a modified build.
