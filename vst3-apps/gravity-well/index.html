GRAVITY WELL

The ultimate bassline synth — if the weight is real.

A bass synthesiser for heavy, potent and aggressive sound — doom, dubstep, industrial, techno — that is just as happy playing an elastic analogue bass in the Moog tradition. Seven filter circuits in two banks, analogue and wavetable oscillators and a deep modulation system give it a range few bass synths have.

At its heart is one control, MASS. Near a black hole clocks run slow, and here every movement in the sound is a clock: turn MASS up and the wobbles slow, deepen and finally freeze at the horizon, while the note stays exactly in tune. At MASS 0 it is simply a very good bass synth.

A sound you can watch

The centre of the panel is the shape space takes around a black hole. The lights orbiting it are the synth’s own modulators — its LFOs, envelopes and random source — each on its own orbit, each slowed by the gravity it sits in. Push MASS and you see the well deepen and the lights slow as you hear the sound thicken; when a light stops on the screen, that movement has stopped in the sound. Drag to turn the view, use the wheel to tilt it.

From elastic to enormous

Gravity Well covers a huge sonic ground, from classic analogue to wild and aggressive modern sounds, and the range comes from how much there is to combine rather than from a menu of fixed models. Seven filter circuits do most of the work: a warm ladder for round, bouncing analogue bass; GROWL and SCREAM for bite and snarl; a clean state-variable; a diode circuit for squelch; a tuned comb for metal; and a vowel bank for talking bass. Two banks run in series, in parallel, or split so the lows and the highs are treated differently — wreck the mids, keep the floor.

Put that behind two oscillators that can each be analogue or wavetable, a sub, noise and a unison disk, drive it before or after the filters, and move it with three envelopes, three LFOs and an eight-slot matrix, and the same instrument goes from a rubbery Moog-style line to a doom drone or a dubstep growl.

The controls

MASS, DEPTH, ENERGY
The three that do the work. MASS is signal energy, velocity and a knob together; DEPTH opens the singularity core; ENERGY drives the whole chain harder.
HORIZON, TIME, SPACE
How big the hole gets, how fast everything runs inside it, and how much room is around it.
Two oscillators + sub + noise
Analogue or wavetable per oscillator, eight tables, sync, phase modulation and ring mod, and a sub that sits one or two octaves down and adds weight rather than mud.
The accretion disk
Unison for width and size: up to eight voices orbiting the note, drifting against each other the way matter circles a black hole.
Ringdown
A resonant body struck by every note, tuned to the way a black hole rings. More mass rings lower and longer — the doom and industrial weight.
The singularity
A protected low core that survives both filters shut and full drive, so the bottom never disappears however hard you push the rest.
Three envelopes, three LFOs
LFOs sync to the host tempo in straight, dotted and triplet divisions. Plus a random source and a chirp that sweeps up faster and faster, like two stars spiralling together. Eight modulation slots on top, and each LFO can drive a target of its own.
Four drives
IDLE BURN, ION, PLASMA and COLLAPSE. Turning one up adds grind, not loudness. It sits before the filters, or after them at a click.
