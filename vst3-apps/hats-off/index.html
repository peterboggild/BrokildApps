HATS OFF

A plate of bronze, and the circuits that copied it. Every hat and cymbal, from those.

A studio hi-hat, an 808 hat and a dubstep crash are not three different machines. They are one plate of bronze set differently — its size, how hard it is held shut, where the stick lands — and the square waves drum machines used to imitate it. Hats Off models the plate, rings its low modes one by one where a cymbal’s pitch lives, and lays a wash over the top the way a real one does; the 808’s circuit sits on the same knob. Then a dub echo, four drives and a compressor. Acoustic hats, rides and crashes, the classic machines, techno, dub and dubstep, from thirty-one controls. No samples.

A plate that rings, closed or free

A drum machine’s hat is six square waves through a band-pass. A real cymbal is a plate with hundreds of modes that do not line up. Hats Off builds both, on one knob.

The plate is rung mode by mode through the low and middle register, because that is where a cymbal’s pitch and size live: SIZE runs from an 8-inch splash to a 24-inch ride, and the display names the lowest mode. Higher up, a real cymbal’s modes crowd and couple until nobody hears them as notes — so there Hats Off lays a wash of noise bands that follow the plate’s own laws: the same decay by frequency, the same tilt with the strike, the same damping as the hat closes. DENSITY walks from a bell-like ping to the continuum of a crash.

STRIKE moves the stick from the bell to the edge. BLOOM is the swell after a crash, when the energy climbs into the top modes. OPEN is the foot: closed, the upper plate damps the lower and the top dies first; half open, SIZZLE lets the plates buzz against each other; CHOKE lets a closed hit stop an open one. And BRONZE turns the plate, a little at a time, into the 808’s circuit.

The display renders the hit every time a control moves, in brass, with its brightness over time in steel: BLOOM shows as a curve that rises after the stick, a closed hat as one that falls at once.

The controls

Thirty-one, in ten sections, left to right in the order the sound meets them.

Metal

The cymbal itself

PITCH ±24 semitones. SIZE 8–24 inches. BRONZE from the 808’s circuit to the plate. DENSITY from ten modes to sixty-four and the wash.

BLOOM the swell. TRASH the china. DECAY 30 ms to 8 s. NOISE for a machine hat.

Hat

Two plates on a pedal

OPEN the foot: how long they ring. SIZZLE the plates buzzing half open. CHICK the pedal note. CHOKE a closed hit stops an open one.

Hit & EQ

Where, and what the mic hears

STRIKE bell, bow, edge. STICK the tick on the ride. CUT the overheads’ high-pass, AIR the top octave.

Era

The machine, the room, the width

GRIT sample rate and bit depth: the 909’s six-bit ROM, the 8-bit Linn. ROOM the overheads. WIDTH spreads the modes and decorrelates the wash; at 0 it is exactly mono.

Echo

The dub

ECHO a ping-pong tape echo, each repeat darker and thinner, send and feedback on one knob. TIME 1/16 to 1/2, locked to the host’s tempo.

Transient

Stick and ring

ATTACK and SUSTAIN, bipolar. The shaper reads the synth’s own envelopes, so its gain cannot ripple.

Drive

Four engines

From Battlestar Overdrive: IDLE BURN, HYPERDRIVE, RAZOR WING, SUPERNOVA. DRIVE and COLOUR. Level-matched, and run at 8x because a cymbal is nearly all top end.

Comp & Out

Roll or pump; touch and keys

COMP and SPEED, 1.5 ms look-ahead. LEVEL under a soft ceiling, VELO, and KEYS: FIXED, GM KIT or CHROMATIC.

All six come from the same controls, so a patch is a whole pair of hats, or a whole cymbal, rather than one sound, and a drum rack that sends the General MIDI notes just works. KEYS set to FIXED plays every note as dialled; CHROMATIC lets the note tune the cymbal.
