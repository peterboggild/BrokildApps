# 09 — Tape stock and the medium (stage 6)

A stock is two things. Its **coating** is the record process: how the particles
answer a field. That is the reference model's job (03–05), run once per coating.
Its **medium** is what the particles, the coating's uniformity and the surface do
along the tape: hiss, modulation noise, spacing, dropouts. Every random property
is a hash of the tape position, so a tape behaves the same every time it is
played.

Source: `Source/Stock.{h,cpp}`, `Source/Medium.{h,cpp}`, the medium's parts of
`Source/Engine.cpp` (`finishCells`, `updateMedium`, `updateShaper`). Bench
section 19.

## Four stocks on three coatings

| stock | coating | operating level (0 VU) | MOL (model) | particles (v / p) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| STUDIO (456 class, default) | studio | 250 nWb/m | 760 nWb/m (fitted to the sheet) | 0.28 × 0.045 µm at 40 % |
| VINTAGE (1960s low output) | vintage | 185 nWb/m | 470 nWb/m | 0.45 × 0.075 µm at 30 % |
| SHEDDING | studio, decayed | 250 nWb/m | 670 nWb/m (12 % of the particles gone) | the studio's, packing × 0.88 |
| BUDGET (echo-machine tape) | budget | 185 nWb/m | 560 nWb/m | 0.35 × 0.06 µm at 30 %, acting four at a time |

The coatings are generated by `reference/mtref --stock=vintage|budget
--tables=... --tablesonly`, then `--pbtables=` at that coating's own 19.05 cm/s
alignment. The data-sheet inputs are class values:

- vintage: Hc 22.3 kA/m, Mr 83.6 kA/m, SFD σ 0.20, interaction 0.50 Hc, S 0.72;
- budget: Hc 24.7 kA/m, Mr 99.5 kA/m, σ 0.18, 0.48 Hc, S 0.75.

The interaction tail ν is fitted to an MOL as for the studio coating. Both fits
hit the tail-shape floor (ν = 1.2) before reaching the MOL asked for (420 and
520 nWb/m). The MOLs above are what the model then does, and the bench holds the
engine to them.

**All four coatings are 14 µm thick.** The real-time slabs (4 × 3.5 µm) and the
head kernels are shared. This is a stated simplification: a thinner coating
would lose bass to thickness loss, and these stocks differ in their particles
and surfaces, not in that.

**The machine is aligned to each fresh stock**, as a technician would align it
on loading the tape:

- bias at that coating's operating point;
- record level so that 0 VU is the stock's operating level;
- record treble for its surface: vintage 0.10 µm and budget 0.15 µm of extra
  spacing, trimmed out by the record EQ.

SHEDDING is the studio tape decayed, so it runs on the studio alignment. Its
lost oxide plays 1.1 dB quieter, and its extra spacing is not trimmed out.
Changing the stock threads a new reel, through the same splice as cutting a new
loop.

## Particle noise (hiss)

A cell of volume V holds N = p V / v particles. Each particle is a single domain
pointing one way or the other along the tape, so the cell's remanence has
variance

    sigma^2 = (1 - m^2) / N      (relative to Mr)

where (1 − m²) is binomial: a saturated cell has every particle aligned and no
noise. One draw is made per cell each time it is recorded. The draw is a hash of
the *unwrapped* cell coordinate, so a loop's next pass is a new draw: the bias
re-randomises the orientations. PARTICLES multiplies N, so ten times the
particles gives 10 dB less hiss. At the top of the range the coating is a
continuum: no noise at all, and silence in gives exactly silence out.

**The slabs need a colour.** A slab holds one value per cell column, so the noise
in it is coherent through the slab's depth. In a real coating the particles are
independent at every depth, and the head hears the shallow ones better, so the
real coating carries more short-wavelength noise. Per slab, the continuous
coating's noise power over the slab's is

    T_l(k)^2 = D * int_top^bot e^(-2ky) dy / ( int_(top+dd)^(bot+dd) e^(-ky) dy )^2

where dd is the slab's effective spacing correction from stage 4. That
correction is a correction for recorded *signals*, so for noise it is undone
here. A 5-tap symmetric FIR along each slab's cells is fitted to T_l, with
G(0) = 1 so the long-wavelength level is untouched. It is refitted when the
speed changes.

Measured against the continuous coating's spectrum, written into the bench from
first principles (particle variance density v/p over track width × thickness²,
every depth independent, spacing 0.3 µm, gap 2.5 µm, the transit box and its
correction FIR; EQ out):

| speed | 1/3-octave bands, 250 Hz – 16 kHz |
|---|---|
| 19.05 cm/s | within 0.58 dB (worst at 16 kHz) |
| 38.1 cm/s | within 0.47 dB |

The tolerance is 1 dB.

| hiss re 0 VU (EQ in, one head, 20 Hz – 20 kHz) | unweighted | A-weighted |
|---|---|---|
| STUDIO, 19.05 cm/s | −58.4 dB | −61.9 dBA |
| STUDIO, 38.1 cm/s | −63.4 dB | −66.6 dBA |
| VINTAGE, 19.05 cm/s | −51.3 dB | −54.7 dBA |
| SHEDDING, 19.05 cm/s | −60.6 dB | −63.6 dBA |
| BUDGET, 19.05 cm/s | −47.2 dB | −50.5 dBA |

A two-track ¼-inch machine on 456 at 19 cm/s measures in the low-to-mid 60s dBA
under 250 nWb/m, which is where the studio stock lands. (The design note's first
guess of −70 dB was optimistic; the particle sizes of a 1970s high-output oxide
put it here.) SHEDDING is a little quieter than STUDIO *relative to its own
0 VU*: it plays everything 1.1 dB lower, and its extra spacing takes some hiss
off the top.

## Modulation noise

The coating's density varies along the tape. A smooth random function of
material position (three octaves at 4, 1 and 0.25 mm) multiplies what is
recorded: (1 + ε). It is a property of the tape, so a loop brings it round every
pass and a reel never repeats it. ε rms by stock: studio 0.25 %, vintage 0.6 %,
budget 0.8 %, shedding 1.2 %, and WEAR raises it ×(1 + 2 wear). Measured skirt
30–300 Hz either side of a 1 kHz tone at 0 VU: studio −58.7 dB, shedding
−36.3 dB re the tone.

## The surface: spacing, roughness, dropouts

A tape lifted from the head by dd is read at height d + dd. A field at d + dd is
the field at d through a Poisson kernel, which along a moving tape is a Cauchy
kernel in time, dd / v wide. Its transform is exp(−k dd), the spacing loss
itself, so the time-domain shaper is exact, not a filter chosen to sound
similar. Per head and track:

- the stock's average extra spacing, plus 0.45 µm × wear;
- roughness: a smooth random lift of the stock's rms, on 60 and 240 µm scales;
- dropouts: up to two candidate events per centimetre of tape, each 0.1–1 mm
  long with an exponentially distributed depth. Some are lifts (spacing); the
  stock's hole fraction are holes in the coating (shed oxide: a broadband dip).
  An event is present when its draw falls under the stock's rate, so more wear
  only ever adds events.

| dropouts | studio | vintage | budget | shedding | per metre × (1 + 12 wear) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| rate /m | 0.3 | 1 | 2 | 6 | |
| mean depth µm | 0.8 | 1.2 | 1.5 | 2.0 | × (1 + wear) |
| holes | 5 % | 15 % | 20 % | 50 % | + 30 % × wear |

Measured: 11 dips past −3 dB at 10 kHz over 3.9 m of SHEDDING tape at
19 cm/s, where the stock's rate and depth distribution predict 15. The
spacing kernel follows exp(−k dd) within 0.054 dB from 5 to 20 kHz, for dd / v
up to a sample. Below that, the Poisson kernel's cusp at DC lives in tails
longer than the kernel: a deep dropout loses up to 0.39 dB less at 2 kHz than
exp(−k dd) says.

**The shaper's taps** are the exact response's inverse transform,
exp(−ω τ) sinc(ω B / 2) over the whole band. Binning the continuous kernel
instead adds a sample's box, which was −0.6 dB at 20 kHz at 96 kHz. The tails'
missing mass goes back in as a raised cosine (a lobe below about 3 kHz), so the
top is exact and DC is exactly unity. The tables are built at prepare, never on
the audio thread. A component that would change nothing below 20 kHz by
0.05 dB (or 0.01 sample) is left out, so the studio tape at 76 cm/s costs
nothing here.

## Azimuth

A playback gap tilted by α from the record gap reads each track along a line
across it. Two effects follow:

- each track reads a box of tape W tan α long: loss sinc(π W tan α / λ);
- the two tracks, S apart, are read S tan α / v apart in time.

The angle per head scales with TRANSPORT CONDITION (a freshly serviced machine
within about a minute of arc; 0.30 / −0.60 / 0.55 × 8 arcmin net at 100 %).
The tape's weave along the guides adds a slow component (REELS, ×1.5 arcmin on
a reel, ×0.5 on a loop). W = 2.0 mm, S = 2.75 mm (two-track ¼ inch).

Measured at CONDITION 100 with the other causes off:

| quantity | measured | exact |
|---|---|---|
| tracks apart in time, 19.05 cm/s | 18.43 µs | 18.48 µs |
| 10 kHz loss at 4.76 cm/s | −4.63 dB | −4.62 dB |

## Wear and ACCUMULATE

WEAR (0–100 %) is how worn the tape is:

- a quarter of the oxide gone at 100 % (−2.5 dB, and relatively more hiss);
- up to 0.45 µm of lift from debris;
- roughness ×4 and dropouts ×13;
- more modulation noise;
- more friction at the heads (SCRAPE × (1 + wear)).

Measured: −2.56 dB at 1 kHz for WEAR 100 % (expected −2.65 dB).

ACCUMULATE: a LOOP wears by its stock's rate on every pass over the heads (studio
2·10⁻⁴, vintage 5·10⁻⁴, budget 6·10⁻⁴, shedding 2·10⁻³ per pass). The wear
belongs to that loop — that stock and that length — and travels with the project
(state attributes `mtWear`, `mtWearStock`, `mtWearLoop`), so a reopened project
finds its loop as worn as it left it; a different loop is a fresh tape. A reel
does not accumulate (its tape passes once).

## Not modelled, said plainly

- The coating's thickness differs between real stocks; here all are 14 µm.
- Bulk-erased versus AC-erased noise. Every cell read has been recorded over,
  so only the AC-erased state appears; the virgin tape at instance start is
  silent until it passes the record head.
- Print-through (dropped in the design, inaudible at loop timescales).
- Dropouts at the record head (only playback lifts).
- Head wear. Azimuth follows the transport's condition, not the heads' age.
- Electronic noise: a playback preamp's hiss would make PARTICLES' top less
  than silent, and the design asked for a silent tape there.
