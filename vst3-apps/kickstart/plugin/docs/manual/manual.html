IN SHORT

Kickstart synthesises kick drums. All of them: the acoustic kit, the drum machines that made the records, and whatever techno, gabber and trap did next.

It does not do it with one simulation per machine. It does it with twenty-one controls that describe what every kick is made of — and then lets you walk from one drum to the next.

THE FIRST TEN SECONDS

Open it, click the big HIT pad, then press > beside the preset name until something makes you grin.

Quick start

INSTALL, HIT, PLAY

1 · INSTALL

Copy Kickstart.vst3 into C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\ and rescan in your DAW. It files under Instrument / Drum. The standalone, Kickstart.exe, needs no installing.

2 · FIRST HIT

Click the HIT pad. The centre is the hardest hit, the rim softer. Space bar works too, and so does any MIDI note.

3 · PICK A PRESET

The < and > buttons step through the banks; the name opens the full list. Presets are level-matched, so you are comparing drums, not volumes.

4 · KEY TRACK

Off, every note plays the same kick — map it to whatever your drum rack sends. On, the note sets the pitch, with C1 at PITCH: load Trap 808 and play a bass line.

5 · KEEP IT

SAVE and LOAD use Documents\Brokild patches\Kickstart.

The idea

ONE KICK, MANY DRUMS

An 808, a 909 and an acoustic kick are not three machines. They are three points in one space.

A kick is a drum whose pitch falls. That is nearly the whole story, and it can be told with a handful of physical axes:

Axis808909Acoustic
How far the pitch falls · SWEEPa littletwo octavesa little
How fast · BENDslowlyin a flashfast
How it rings · DECAY, CURVElong and pureshortershort, damped
What it is made of · WAVE, SKINsinetriangle-isha real head
What hits it · CLICK, TONEbarelya noise clicka beater
Where it is · GRIT, ROOMdrydrya room

Move along any one of those and you walk from one drum to another. The recipes on page 11 are walks like that. Everything after — transient, drive, compression — is what a producer would do to the drum next.

The idea04

Section 1 of 7

BODY

PITCH25–160 Hz

Where the kick settles. The display shows the note name beside it, so you can tune the kick to the song.

SWEEP0–48 semitones

How far above PITCH the hit starts. An 808 a little, a 909 a lot, gabber more still.

BEND2–300 ms

How fast the pitch falls. Short is a snap, long is a zap.

DECAY50 ms–4 s

The time to fall 60 dB. The display prints the result, so “60 dB in 420 ms” means exactly that.

THE FASTEST WAY TO A DIFFERENT DRUM

SWEEP and BEND together. Most of the difference between an 808 and a 909 is how far the pitch falls and how quickly. Move those two first, and everything else is detail.

CURVE0–100 %

At 0 the kick dies the way a struck drum does, exponentially. At 100 it holds full level and then is gone — the modern sub.

WAVEsine → square

Sine, through triangle (the 909, around 35 %), to a rounded square. More harmonics, more presence on small speakers.

SKIN0–100 %

From an electronic oscillator to a real drum head. It adds the circular membrane’s inharmonic modes and the tension glide: a real head starts sharp the harder you hit it, then settles.

Sections 2 and 3 of 7

HIT & ERA

Hit · the beater

CLICK

The beater’s level: how much of the moment of impact you hear on top of the drum.

TONE

What the beater is. Low is a felt thud, then a wooden knock, then the 909’s noise click, and at the top a digital tick.

Era · when and where

GRIT

Lowers the sample rate and the bit depth. Around 30 % sounds like the 12-bit machines — SP-12, Linn 9000, MPC60. Around 55 % it is the 8-bit DMX and LinnDrum.

ROOM

The air around a real kit. Feed it into DRIVE and it stops being a room and becomes a techno rumble.

WHY ERA IS ITS OWN SECTION

The sample machines of the eighties did not sound the way they did because of their kicks. They sounded that way because of their converters. GRIT is the converter; the drum is still yours.

Hit & Era06

Section 4 of 7

TRANSIENT

ATTACKbipolar

More or less of the first milliseconds. Up for a kick that cuts through a mix; down to round it off. Centre does nothing.

SUSTAINbipolar

More or less of the tail. Down to tighten a boomy kick without touching DECAY; up to bring out the body and the room.

Transient shapers usually have to guess. They follow the audio with two envelope detectors and compare them, and on a low kick those detectors ride on the waveform itself — the gain wobbles at the pitch of the drum.

Kickstart does not have to guess. It is a synthesiser, so it knows its own envelopes, and the shaper reads those directly.

NO RIPPLE

Because nothing is detected from the audio, there is nothing to ripple. What is left over on a low kick measures −79 dB.

TRY THIS

Load Psytrance Tick: ATTACK is up and SUSTAIN is down, which is most of why it is all attack. Now swap them round.

Section 5 of 7

DRIVE

Four engines, lifted from Brokild’s own Battlestar Overdrive.

EngineWhat it does
IDLE BURNA warm, asymmetric tube. Weight and a second harmonic.
HYPERDRIVEHarmonic injection. Adds harmonics above the sub, so a kick that lives at 45 Hz still reads on a phone speaker.
RAZOR WINGTanh into a hard clip. Flat-topped and mean — this is gabber.
SUPERNOVAFold, crush and chaos. Broken on purpose.

DRIVE

How hard the engine is pushed.

COLOUR

The drive’s low-pass. Fully open is no filter at all; pull it down for a darker, rounder distortion.

LEVEL-MATCHED

Every engine is matched by measurement, so turning DRIVE up changes the character, not the loudness — within 2.4 dB across every engine and every setting. Judge it by ear, not by volume.

Sections 6 and 7 of 7

COMP & OUT

Comp

COMP

Threshold and ratio on one knob, with its own make-up gain, so turning it up makes the kick denser, not quieter.

SPEED

Slow lets the hit punch through before the compressor grabs the body. Fast flattens the whole thing and pumps. The compressor looks 1.5 ms ahead, so even fast it catches the front.

RETRIGGER

Hit it again while it is still ringing and the old kick is choked over 3 ms, the way a real voice would be, rather than clicking.

Out

LEVEL

Output level. A soft ceiling holds everything under 0 dBFS however hard the section before it is pushed.

VELO

How much a hard hit differs from a soft one: louder, brighter, and bending further.

KEY TRACK

Off: every note plays the same kick. On: the MIDI note sets the pitch, with C1 = PITCH, so the kick plays in tune as an 808 bass line.

THE DISPLAY & THE PAD

THE HIT

The whole kick, drawn in orange, redrawn every time a control moves. You see a change before you hear it.

THE PITCH

The amber curve is the pitch falling to PITCH. The top line reads where it settles, the note, where it starts and the decay time.

PAD & METERS

Click the pad: centre hardest, rim softer. Space and MIDI work too. OUT is the output level; GR is the compressor at work.

RECIPES

From one style to another, a few knobs at a time.

808 → 909

  1. Start on 808 Boom.
  2. Raise SWEEP towards 24 semitones and shorten BEND to about 20 ms.
  3. WAVE to 35 %: the triangle.
  4. CLICK up, TONE towards the noise click. Shorten DECAY to taste.

ELECTRONIC → ACOUSTIC

  1. SKIN up past 50 %.
  2. SWEEP down to a few semitones.
  3. TONE down, towards felt or wood.
  4. A little ROOM. Then SUSTAIN down if it rings too long.

TECHNO RUMBLE

  1. A 909-ish kick with a longer DECAY.
  2. ROOM up to about 60 %.
  3. DRIVE up, IDLE BURN: the room goes through the drive.
  4. COLOUR down until the tail rumbles instead of hisses. Some COMP.

GABBER

  1. SWEEP up high, three octaves.
  2. CURVE up, so the kick holds instead of dying.
  3. WAVE up, then RAZOR WING with DRIVE near the top.
  4. CLICK and TONE up. Play it at 180.

ANY KICK → A PLAYABLE BASS

  1. CURVE up past 50 %, DECAY to two seconds or more.
  2. HYPERDRIVE with a little DRIVE, so it reads on a phone.
  3. KEY TRACK on. C1 plays at PITCH.
  4. Keep SWEEP modest, or every note starts with a zap.

CLEAN → 1988

  1. GRIT to about 30 % for 12-bit, 55 % for 8-bit.
  2. A touch of IDLE BURN, COLOUR down a little.
  3. COMP up with a slow SPEED, so the hit punches through.
  4. Put it under a sampled break.
