The idea
01

A war between sixteen copies of one machine

Every great analogue drone has the same secret: many voices that are supposed to be identical, and are not. Sixteen oscillators built from the same schematic never agree — each one is a few cents off, a few degrees warmer, a little slower to wake. Clone Wars builds that disagreement on purpose.

The console holds sixteen clones, each a complete voice: an oscillator with four waveforms and four footages, an analogue-modelled filter, its own LFO, two looping envelopes, drift, pan and a fader. They stand in two armies of eight — Army A on the left, Army B on the right — with a command column between them. The two armies can carry two entirely different sounds, and THE WAR crossfade slides the mix from one to the other over anything from fifty milliseconds to five minutes.

What makes the machine breathe is the imperfection engine. TOLERANCE scatters component values across the sixteen clones, so no two strips are quite the same instrument. DRIFT lets every oscillator wander slowly around its pitch. ENTRAIN pulls the clones of an army gently into phase with each other, the way oscillators sharing a power rail fall into step. TIDE is a conductor moving over minutes, leaning on the whole orchestra at once. Between them, a held chord is never static: it swells, beats, locks and unlocks.

Everything on the panel is deterministic. A seed number is a whole patch — the same number is the same drone on any machine — and the panel morphs continuously between two seeds. Even the scratches are deterministic: the hull records its service hours and accumulates dents, chips and grime as it is played, and two numbers in your DAW project replay the whole damage history. There is no off switch for that. There is only REPAIR.

Thirty seconds of instructions

A fresh instance is silent — a VST3 has manners. Give it a note and it becomes a monster.

Two minutes, no installer

A VST3 plugin is a folder you copy into place. That is the whole ceremony.

StepAction
1Unzip Clone-Wars-VST3-win64.zip (right-click → Extract All). Inside: the Clone Wars.vst3 folder, the standalone Clone Wars.exe, this manual, and a readme.
2Copy the whole Clone Wars.vst3 folder — not just a file from inside it — to C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\. A sub-folder such as …\VST3\Brokild\ is fine; hosts look inside. Windows asks for administrator permission; that is normal.
3Rescan plugins in your DAW (Ableton Live: Preferences → Plug-Ins → Rescan). Clone Wars appears as an instrument, by Brokild. Put it on a MIDI track and hold a chord.
orSkip the DAW: run Clone Wars.exe. It picks its audio and MIDI devices from its own options menu and has the field keyboard built in.
If the plugin window is blank or shows an error page: install the free Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime from developer.microsoft.com/microsoft-edge/webview2 and reopen the window. The interface is drawn with WebView2, which every current Windows 10 and 11 ships with, but which can be missing on older or heavily stripped installations. The audio engine runs either way.
If Windows refuses to load the plugin and mentions an Application Control policy, that is Smart App Control. It judges each file from a cloud reputation lookup and can block one unsigned binary while loading another happily. Most machines do not have it enabled at all; Windows Security → App & browser control shows whether yours does.

04

Anatomy of a clone

Each of the sixteen strips is the same soldier, read top to bottom. The row labels stand on both wings of the console, so nothing is ever more than a glance away.

RowWhat it does
LEVEL VUA real needle VU with real ballistics, one per clone — sixteen needles leaning together is half the show.
NOTE 1·2·3Which of the three held MIDI notes this clone follows (chapter 06).
WAVESAW / PULSE / TRI / SINE — band-limited analogue-modelled cores.
FOOTAGE64' / 32' / 16' / 8' / 4' / 2' — organ-style octave, three below unison to two above. 32' shakes the floor; 64' is felt more than heard; the low ranks can be pulled to mono centre on the bus (chapter 09).
TUNEMicrotune, a few cents either way. Where the beating between clones lives. The engine-room SPREAD knob fans all sixteen at once.
CUTOFF · RESThe clone's own analogue-modelled filter — its circuit is chosen per army by the TEMPER switch (chapter 05).
LFO WAVE · RATESIN / TRI / SQR / S&H per clone, with its own rate — sixteen LFOs at sixteen speeds is what makes the field shimmer.
LFO→AMP · LFO→FLTHow much of that LFO reaches the clone's level and its filter cutoff.
ENV FLT · ENV AMPTwo one-knob envelopes. The knob is a journey: hard left is fast attack / fast decay (percussive), noon is fast / slow (struck, then breathing), hard right is slow / slow (tidal). One knob, the whole family of shapes.
ENV LOOPThe envelope self-retriggers — the strip breathes on its own, its period set by the envelope travel.
DRIFTHow far this clone wanders around its pitch, scaled by the command column's DRIFT master.
PAN · FADERPosition and level in the army's mix.
M · SMute and solo, per clone.
Row warfare — commanding sixteen strips at once
Shift-clickLocks any combination of controls in one row into a group — drag one and the whole group moves. Lock four LFO RATE knobs and sweep them together; the lock ring marks the members.
Ctrl-clickLocks a whole half-row — the clicked army's eight — in unison: every control snaps to the clicked one's value and they move as one. Repeat the gesture to release the side.
Shift-ctrl-clickThe same half-row lock, but every control keeps its own value — the intervals travel together. Armies are independent: lock A's cutoffs in unison while B's stay scattered.
Lock tool (rails)The same, from the rails: each side label carries a lock tool beside the dice and the fan. Click locks that army's half-row keeping intervals; ctrl-click locks it in unison on the army's first clone.
Dice (rails)Rerolls a whole row from the seed — locked controls roll together.
Fan (rails)Draws a ramp across clones 1→16 — a filter sunrise, a pan sweep, a stagger of envelope speeds, in one click.
WAR PAINTVery faint colour rings sort the rows into families — oscillator, filter, LFO, envelope, mix. The ring of a row lights a little brighter while you touch it, then fades back into the paint.

The console · centre
05

The command column

Everything that rules the whole machine stands in the centre, between the armies. Top to bottom:

StationFunction
SEED · MORPHTwo red LED seed numbers — A and B — and a MORPH knob that glides the whole console continuously between them (chapter 07).
PATCHES · RANDOMThe seed browser, five families deep — and a reroll of everything from a fresh seed.
ENGINECycles the render tier: LOW / HQ / XHQ (chapter 10).
TOLERANCEComponent scatter. At zero the sixteen clones are one perfect machine; opened up, every strip is a slightly different instrument — filter, envelope and LFO all wander from spec, deterministically, from the unit's own serial seed.
TIDEThe conductor. A random walk moving over minutes that leans on the whole orchestra — level, cutoff, pitch — the way a drone piece swells without anyone touching anything.
ENTRAIN A · BPhase pull inside each army. Low, the clones beat freely against each other; high, they fall into step like oscillators sharing a rail — the beating slows, thickens, and locks.
DRIFTMaster depth for every clone's pitch wander.
TEMPER A · BEach army's filter circuit: GROWL — a Sallen-Key voice with diode squash, gnarly and bass-keeping; SCREAM — the same circuit pushed far harder, howling on its own harmonics near the top; LADDER — a four-pole transistor ladder, round, fat, Moog-mannered. One switch turns an army into a different animal.
LATCH A · BPer-army note latch — released notes keep sounding for that army. The keyboard's HOLD switch drives both at once.
HELD N1·N2·N3Lamps for the three note slots — lit while a slot is occupied.
THE WARThe A↔B crossfade, equal-power, with SLEW — the fader's travel time, from fifty milliseconds to five minutes — and the MASTER level beside it (chapter 08).
THE RANKSThe sync/desync slider. Left of centre pulls every continuous control toward its army's average — hard left is sixteen perfect copies of one machine; right of centre exaggerates the differences, up to full mutiny. Non-destructive: the knobs never move, and dead centre is always exactly your patch. Discrete rows and the faders are untouched; the armies are treated independently. Double-click to recentre.
BACK PANELThe effects: SPRING (dwell, mix, and FREEZE — an infinite spring for drone sustain), BBD bucket-brigade chorus (rate, depth), TAPE echo (60 ms–1.2 s, feedback, mix), and DRIVE for the whole bus.
ENGINE ROOM · WAR PAINT · KEYSThe under-the-hood hatch (chapter 09), the row colour rings, and the field keyboard (chapter 06).
The armies are two different sounds, not one sound twice. Give Army A a 32'-heavy LADDER abyss and Army B a screaming 4' swarm, park THE WAR in the middle, and the machine plays a duet with itself. Then set SLEW to minutes and let the war decide.

06
Playing it
06

Three notes, sixteen clones, one keyboard

Most drones are one note. Clone Wars listens to three at once — and lets every clone choose which one it obeys.

The machine keeps three note slots, filled in order of arrival: the first held note takes slot 1, the second slot 2, the third slot 3. A fourth note steals the oldest slot. Each strip's NOTE 1·2·3 buttons say which slot that clone follows — so a held three-note chord can be voiced any way you like across the armies: all of Army A on the bass note, Army B split across the fifth and the octave, one lone clone riding the top. When a clone's slot is empty it falls back to the lowest occupied one, so the drone never drops a soldier.

LATCH A and LATCH B hold notes after release, per army. With latch off the machine goes quiet when you let go, like an instrument; with latch on it stands where you left it, like a monument. Fresh instances open with latch off and make no sound until played — a VST3 has manners. For an installation drone that must sound with no input at all, automate the host parameter drone: it wakes the machine on its base pitch, no MIDI required.

The field keyboard

Press KEYS and a three-octave field keyboard (C2–C5) floats up into the keyboard bay beneath the console — a two-second landing, drifting in from below the deck with its shadow growing on the hull, spaceship fashion. Press KEYS again and it departs the same way. The bay is its own deck: the keyboard covers no controls while deployed.

MouseClick to play, drag across for glissandi.
Computer keysA W S E D F T G Y H U J K — home row plays an octave, black keys on the row above.
HOLDThe latch switch on the keyboard plate — engages LATCH A and B together, and mirrors them both ways.
LiveryThe keys wear the hull's own paint: the black keys are panel blue, the whites a lighter blue of the same metal. Unusual for a keyboard. This is an unusual machine.
Voicing recipe: hold a low C, a G above it, and a high C. Set Army A's clones to NOTE 1, Army B's alternating 2 and 3. Now THE WAR is not just a timbre crossfade — it is a harmonic one, marching from the root to the interval and back.

Patches
07

Seeds, families, and the morph

A seed number is a patch. Not a pointer to one — the patch itself, generated from the number, bit-identical on every machine, forever.

Dial a seed and the entire console jumps to it: all sixteen strips, the tempers, the imperfection engine, the effects. Each seed belongs to a family chosen first, so the category and the sound always agree:

FamilyCharacter
ABYSS32' and 16' footage, LADDER tempers, slow tides — the bottom of the ocean.
SWARMHigh footages, sharp tolerances, fast scattered LFOs — a field of insects with opinions.
ENGINESPulse-heavy, entrained, mechanical — the hum of a hull under power.
CATHEDRALWide, spring-frozen, slow envelopes — sacred space in a box.
RUSTDetuned, driven, S&H-modulated — beautiful decay.

The command column carries two seeds at once — the red LED numbers A and B — and the MORPH knob glides the whole console continuously between them. Every knob value travels; the selectors switch over at the halfway point. A slow morph between an ABYSS and a RUST is a composition in itself. RANDOM deals a fresh seed; PATCHES browses all of them by family.

A seed is a postcard. Tell a friend "CLONE WARS, seed 042" and they hold your exact drone. Tweak any knob afterwards and the sound is yours — the DAW project saves every parameter as it stands, and the seed display still shows where you started.
The war
08

A crossfade with a strategy

THE WAR is the horizontal fader in the command column: hard left is Army A alone, hard right is Army B, equal-power all the way across. What makes it a weapon is SLEW — the fader's own travel time. At the bottom the war snaps in fifty milliseconds; at the top, a single drag takes five minutes to arrive, the two armies trading ground so slowly the change is felt before it is heard. Automate the target from the DAW and the slew still shapes the journey — the fader on the panel shows the war's true position, not the order it was given.

Under the hood
09

The engine room

The front panel is for playing. The ENGINE ROOM button opens the hatch to everything else — four bolts, one dark room.

StationFunction
BASE A · BASE BEach army's base pitch — where the clones sit when the drone parameter drives them, and the floor the note slots transpose from. Two armies, two keys.
GLIDEPortamento between held notes, up to two seconds — the drone way of changing chords.
SPREADFans all sixteen microtunes symmetrically — the one-knob version of detuning every strip by hand.
KBD A · KBD BFilter key-tracking per army — how much the cutoff follows the note.
SCATTER · LFO SYNCPanel fitted, wiring scheduled: SCATTER will re-randomise all sixteen LFO phases, LFO SYNC will lock the LFO rates to the host clock. In this build the LFO phases come from the seed and free-run.
WIDTHMaster stereo width, from mono through unity to wide.
BUS SATAnalogue bus soft-clip with automatic gain compensation — glue at low settings, weld at high ones.
BASS MONOPulls footages 16' and below to centre — the low ranks stand firm while the high ranks fight across the stereo field.
HPFA gentle infrasonic high-pass on the master — sixteen drifting oscillators can conspire below hearing; this keeps the conspiracy off your woofers.
MODULE BAYFour empty slots. Future plug-in modules dock here — the bay is real, the modules are coming.
The output is always safe. Underneath everything sits a transparent soft ceiling: it does nothing at all until the mix approaches full scale, then rounds the peaks instead of clipping them. Nothing this machine can do — not sixteen screaming filters, not a frozen spring into full drive — puts a hard edge above full scale into your monitors.

Rendering
10

LOW · HQ · XHQ

The ENGINE button cycles three render tiers. They change how hard the nonlinear stages — the filters and saturators, where aliasing is born — work per sample:

TierWhat it means
LOWNo oversampling. The full console at minimum CPU — for sketching on a laptop with the fans off. Still band-limited oscillators, still the soft ceiling.
HQNonlinearities run 2× oversampled. The default: transparent for everything short of the most violent SCREAM settings.
XHQ4× oversampled nonlinearities — the measured aliasing floor of the whole engine. Costs real CPU with sixteen clones running.
Bounces are always XHQ. When the host renders offline — an export, a freeze, a bounce-in-place — the engine ignores the panel switch and runs XHQ. Play at LOW all day; the record is always made at full quality.
The living hull
11

The hull remembers

Every instance of Clone Wars keeps an odometer — UNIT AGE, top right, in hours of service. While the unit plays, it ages. And as it ages, it scars.

Dings, chips, paint peel, rust breakthrough, smears and fingerprints accumulate on the panel with use — photographic damage, placed deterministically from the unit's own serial, so your scars are yours and stay exactly where they fell. The age and the damage history are saved per instance, inside the DAW project: the Clone Wars in your old project is old, the one you added today is factory fresh. Hard sessions at high level scar faster than quiet ones.

There is no off switch for any of this. There is only repair: shift-click the odometer and the SERVICE BAY hatch opens. REPAIR restores the hull to factory condition and starts the service clock again — new panel, day zero. TOUR does the opposite: it simulates a year of hard touring, right now, if you want an instrument with a past the day you meet it.
