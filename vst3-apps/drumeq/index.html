DRUMEQ

Every mic of the kit. One plug-in on each track, one kit across all of them.

A kick wants its thump and its beater, a snare its body and its crack, an overhead its air and no mud. DrumEQ knows which drum it is on: pick KICK IN, SNARE TOP, TOM 3 or OH on the top row and its four bands are named and ranged for that drum, with a gate, a compressor and five presets each, written from common mixing practice. The instances share a kit, so your drummer’s whole kit can be saved and loaded at once, and a MASTER panel can tune every drum from one window. FIND DRUM listens to the song and sets the input level, the gate and the drum’s note. Zero latency, made for rock and metal.

Eleven drums, one plug-in

Each mic is its own track, and a DAW gives a plug-in one input. So DrumEQ goes on every drum track, and the drum row tells it which one.

The SOURCE row — KICK IN, KICK OUT, SNR TOP, SNR BOT, TOM 1–3, HI-HAT, RIDE, ROOM, OH — relabels and re-ranges the four EQ bands for that drum: THUMP, BOX, BEATER and CLICK on a kick in, BODY, BOX, CRACK and AIR on a snare, LOW, MUD, HARSH and AIR on the overheads. Each band’s frequency knob covers only what that band means on that drum, so a kick’s THUMP runs 35–160 Hz and its BEATER 1.5–9 kHz. The gate, compressor and filters start where that drum wants them. The choice of drum is always yours: DrumEQ never guesses what is on a track.

The chain is IN → GATE → FILTERS → EQ → COMPRESSOR → OUT, with no latency. The EQ bands follow their analog curve to the top of the audio range — a +6 dB bell at 12 kHz lands 0.49 dB from it at 44.1 kHz, where the textbook design is 2.72 dB off — and with everything neutral the output is the input, bit for bit.

Let it listen

Most drum tracks are recorded quiet, and not in proportion to each other. FIND DRUM fixes the input instead of chasing the thresholds.

1 · Level

Every track at the same level

Press FIND DRUM while the song plays. In six seconds it finds the drum’s own hits, tells them from the spill of the rest of the kit, and sets IN so a typical hit peaks at −12 dBFS — where the presets expect it.

2 · Gate

Between the spill and the drum

The gate threshold goes between the loudest spill and the quietest hit, heard through the gate’s own key band.

3 · Note

The body band on the drum

On kick, snare and toms it finds the drum’s note and puts the body band on it. Hi-hat, ride, room and overheads get SET LEVEL: steps 1 and 2.

Keep track level

Faders stay where they are

With KEEP TRACK LEVEL on, OUT gives back what IN took, so a mix already balanced on the faders does not move.

The same snare recorded 34 dB apart was trimmed +27.9 and −6.1 dB and came out at −9.0 dBFS both times.

One kit, one panel

The DrumEQs in a session talk to each other.

LINK joins a track to the kit. SAVE KIT stores every drum in one file — your drummer’s kit, ready for the next song — and LOAD KIT sends each drum to its tracks at once. Six factory kits: STANDARD, CLASSIC ROCK, MODERN METAL, EXTREME METAL, VINTAGE ROCK and NATURAL.

MASTER turns one track’s panel blue. The drum row then picks which drum you edit, not what the track is: that track keeps playing its own drum, marked HOME, and every linked track on the drum you edit follows, while the display and meters show what that track hears. Eleven panels become one. Each track still stores its own settings, so removing the master or reopening the set changes nothing.
