Quick start

ONE PLUG-IN PER DRUM

WHAT IT IS FOR

Each mic of an acoustic kit wants different things: a kick wants its thump and its beater, a snare its body and crack, an overhead its air and no mud. DrumEQ knows which drum it is on and labels and ranges its bands for that drum, with five presets each written from common mixing practice.

1 · INSTALL

Copy DrumEQ.vst3 into C:\Program Files\Common Files\VST3\ and rescan. It files under Effect / EQ. DrumEQ.exe is the same effect as a standalone.

2 · PICK THE DRUM

Click the drum on the top row: KICK IN, KICK OUT, SNR TOP, SNR BOT, TOM 1–3, HI-HAT, RIDE, ROOM or OH. The choice is always yours — DrumEQ never guesses what is on a track.

3 · PICK A PRESET

< and > step through the drum’s five presets; the name opens the list. SAVE stores your own.

4 · LET IT LISTEN

Play the song and press FIND DRUM. In six seconds it sets the input level, the gate and the body band. Page 5.

5 · MAKE IT A KIT

LINK on every track, then SAVE KIT. With MASTER one panel tunes every drum. Page 6.

The controls

IN, FILTERS, EQ, GATE, COMP, OUT

The sound goes IN → GATE → FILTERS → EQ → COMPRESSOR → OUT, with no latency. Every switch fades over 10 ms and every knob glides over 20 ms, so nothing clicks; with everything neutral the output is the input, bit for bit.

IN

TRIM −24 to +36 dB, so a quiet mic can be brought up to where the presets expect it. POL flips polarity (Snare Bottom ships flipped).

FILTERS

HPF 10 Hz–1 kHz and LPF 1–22 kHz, each OFF, 12 or 24 dB per octave.

OUT

TRIM −36 to +24 dB.

EQ

Four bands with FREQ, GAIN ±18 dB and Q. Band 1 and band 4 switch between SHELF and BELL. Each band is named and ranged for the drum: on a kick THUMP, BOX, BEATER and CLICK; on a snare BODY, BOX, CRACK and AIR. Drag the numbered nodes on the display, or turn the knobs.

The bands are designed to match the analog curve up to the top of the audio range — a +6 dB bell at 12 kHz is 0.49 dB off at 44.1 kHz, where the textbook design is 2.72 dB off.

GATE

THRESH, RANGE (how far it closes), ATTACK, HOLD, RELEASE. KEY LO and KEY HI band-pass what the gate listens to, so a tom gate opens on the tom and not the snare beside it.

COMPRESSOR

THRESH, RATIO, ATTACK, RELEASE, KNEE, MAKEUP, MIX (parallel compression) and KEY HPF, which keeps the low end from pumping the rest — fully left is off. AUTO REL lets the release follow the material.

Each band’s frequency knob only covers the range that band means on that drum: a kick’s THUMP runs 35–160 Hz, its BEATER 1.5–9 kHz; a snare’s CRACK 1.8–9 kHz. The three toms share their presets but each keeps its own body range, from 40–180 Hz on the floor tom to 80–320 Hz on the rack tom.

WHY ONE PLUG-IN, NOT ELEVEN

Each mic is its own track, and a DAW gives a plug-in one input. So DrumEQ runs once per track, the drum row relabels it, and the instances talk to each other through a shared kit (page 6).

The drums04

Find drum · set level

LET IT LISTEN

Most drum tracks are recorded quiet, and not in proportion to each other. Chasing every threshold down to a quiet track leaves the knobs at the bottom and the presets meaning nothing. So FIND DRUM fixes the input instead.

1 · THE LEVEL

Press it while the song plays (or the drummer plays); it listens for six seconds, or until you press it again. It finds the drum’s own hits, tells them from the spill of the rest of the kit, and sets IN so a typical hit peaks at −12 dBFS. Every track then arrives at the level the presets were written for.

2 · THE GATE

The gate threshold goes between the loudest spill and the quietest hit, in the gate’s own key band, clear of its 4 dB hysteresis.

3 · THE NOTE

On kick, snare and toms it also finds the drum’s note and puts the body band on it. Hi-hat, ride, room and overheads have no single note, so their button reads SET LEVEL and does steps 1 and 2.

KEEP TRACK LEVEL

With this on, OUT gives back what IN took, so a mix already balanced on the faders does not move. Off, the kit arrives normalised.

MEASURED

The same snare recorded 34 dB apart (×0.01 and ×0.5) was trimmed +27.9 and −6.1 dB, and came out at −9.0 dBFS both times. A kick found at 52.1 Hz for 52, a floor tom at 82.2 for 82.

The kit · master

ONE KIT, ONE PANEL

LINK

Every DrumEQ in the session with LINK on is part of one kit. A track switched to a drum the kit already holds takes the kit’s settings for it.

SAVE KIT · LOAD KIT

From any track. SAVE KIT stores every drum in one .drumkit file — your drummer’s kit, ready for the next song. LOAD KIT sends each drum to its linked tracks at once. Six factory kits: STANDARD, CLASSIC ROCK, MODERN METAL, EXTREME METAL, VINTAGE ROCK and NATURAL.

MASTER

With LINK on, press MASTER on any track and its panel turns blue. The drum row now picks which drum you edit; this track keeps playing its own drum, marked HOME. Every linked track on the drum you edit follows at once, and the display and meters show what that track hears. FIND DRUM works from here too. One master at a time; pressing MASTER elsewhere takes the role over.

GOOD TO KNOW

Each track stores what it receives in its own settings, so deleting the master track or reopening the set changes nothing. A drum with no track yet can still be set up; it is kept in the kit. Host automation on a track wins over the master there, and the master’s changes reach the DAW as that track’s own (undo steps).
