BATTLESTAR
OVERDRIVE

An overdrive named after the band, made with the band.

Max Christensen had the idea and it was too good to leave alone: a Battlestar Overdrive for Battlestar Overdrive. So here it is, built with his blessing and made to live inside his universe rather than beside it. Eight drive engines on one knob, running from a polite tube to a fold-crush-chaos cascade with no musical justification whatsoever; a CRT that tells you which one you are on, speeds its starfield up as you climb, goes to warp on the seventh and blows up a star on the eighth. It is very good at ruining things. That is the point.

Go and listen to the band

This is a tribute first and a plugin second.

Battlestar Overdrive is a one-man band out of Copenhagen that crash-lands somewhere between a late-night discotheque and a sweaty basement venue: garage rock swagger welded to soul, disco and indie electronica, played on a guitar with some very determined drums and no interest whatsoever in tidying it up. It is bonkers, hyper-charged and unreasonably optimistic about it — for the misfits, the outsiders, and anyone ready to ride a wave of space-travelling rock and roll.

Put it on. The plugin makes more sense afterwards, and so, probably, does everything else.

Collaborations like this are rare here. This one happened in September 2026 because the idea arrived fully formed and could not be argued with.

Eight engines, ordered from polite to unhinged

Which is what putting them on one knob promises.

  1. IDLE BURN — soft asymmetric tube. Warmth and a second harmonic. The one you leave on.
  2. ION DRIVE — the classic soft knee. Transparent until pushed, then obliging.
  3. PLASMA COIL — tape saturation with memory. It remembers the last thing you hit it with.
  4. AFTERBURNER — asymmetric fuzz, squashed. Where it starts sounding like a decision.
  5. RAZOR WING — tanh into a hard clip. Flat-topped and mean.
  6. WARP FOLD — a wavefolder. Past a point, louder stops meaning brighter and starts meaning stranger.
  7. HYPERDRIVE — harmonic injection. Chebyshev, so it adds harmonics that were never in there.
  8. SUPERNOVA — fold, crush and chaos. No musical justification is offered or implied.
Every engine is level-matched by a table the plugin measures for itself at startup, so turning THRUST up changes what it sounds like rather than simply how loud it is. A hardcoded table goes stale the first time a curve is retuned, silently — this one cannot.

The tube is part of the instrument

Everything the plugin says, it says inside the screen. There are no numbers printed on the metal.

Width that survives a mono fold

ANTITHRUST, and why it works after the drive rather than before it.

Distortion is the enemy of a stereo image: two slightly different signals through two shapers make wildly different harmonics, and the picture smears instead of widening. So ANTITHRUST sits after the engine, and it only ever adds decorrelated width derived from the middle of the signal — it never touches the middle itself.

Two things follow, and both are measured rather than asserted. Whatever the knob is doing, the mono sum does not move — 0.02 dB across the entire sweep — so nothing collapses on a club rig or a phone. And an incoming stereo image survives, because what arrived is passed through and only added to, never replaced.

Mono-exact and level-exact cannot both hold: adding decorrelated width genuinely raises total energy, and the only way to hold the level while widening is to pull the middle down — which is exactly what breaks the mono sum. This one picks mono.

SPACE is a journey, not a menu

One knob across four effects, and they overlap.

early

Tape delay

The time traces 30 ms out to 600 and back to 30 in one arch, so the knob passes through the useful range instead of ending on it.

middle

A large hall

Fades in underneath, and its decay grows as you go.

three quarters

Shimmer

An octave folds back into the room. The hall shortens as it rises — a six-second tail has no loop gain left to lend.

top

Harmonic tremolo

Takes over, and it stops pretending to be a room at all.

The fuel gauge means it

A joke, and a load-bearing one.

The tank goes down one notch for every five seconds you actually play. Silence is free. It is not metering anything and it is not modelling a power supply — it is there because the panel has a fuel gauge on it, and a gauge that never moves is a lie.

With Autorefill lit — the default — it tops itself up at the notch above empty and nothing ever happens to your sound. With it dark, about seventy seconds of continuous playing empties the tank, the engine flames out over the following five seconds, and the tube blinks FUEL EMPTY in red at you.

Press Autorefill. That is the whole recovery procedure.

What the metal does not have room for

The panel decal came first, and nothing the plugin does was allowed to need a control the metal does not have.

That is why there is no quality switch, no oversampling menu and no output trim: the plugin decides those for you. It runs at 4× oversampling all the time, worst-case aliasing across every engine measures −99.8 dB, and the oversampler is flat to ±0.002 dB out to 19 kHz — which costs about 2.5 % of one core and is not worth a menu.

Two controls exist that no knob fits, so they live in your host's automation list only: SPACE WET, an overall level for the SPACE section whose centre is exactly what the SPACE knob does on its own, and SPACE SYNC, which locks the delay to the host clock.
