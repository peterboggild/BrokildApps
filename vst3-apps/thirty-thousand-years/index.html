Thirty Thousand Years

An instrument for humanity's darkest hour.

Four different ways of making sound, all of them available at once, in a shared environment whose feedback loop takes part in generating the sound instead of only treating it. A modulation network that listens to the engine's own output and answers it. And one control that carries the whole thing from one condition to another over anything from a few seconds to half an hour.

It is named for how long spent nuclear fuel stays dangerous. The panel is an operational console built to outlive its civilization: severe, precise, tactile, and the same shape wherever you are in it.

A place, not a patch

The four strata, and the room that helps make the sound.

Most synthesizers give you one way of making sound and a chain of effects to put it through. Thirty Thousand Years gives you four, running together, and then an environment that is part of the instrument rather than a finish on it. Each stratum is a complete engine with its own filter, its own envelopes and its own place in the mixer. Any of them can excite any of the others.

The consequential control is at the end of the feedback loop, and it is called RETURNS TO. Send the loop back to the mix and you have an echo. Send it into STRUCTURE's exciter and the room is playing the body. Send it into MEMORY's capture and the loop is being remembered. Send it into SIGNAL's ring modulator and it multiplies whatever is already there. The loop has its own high-pass, low-pass, frequency shift, saturation and damping, so it can be run near unity without running away.

The four strata

Four theses about how sound is made, in one instrument.

Stratum one

MASS

Virtual analogue. Two oscillators, a sub, a ladder or state-variable filter, and a beat set in hertz rather than cents — so a drone at 55 Hz and one at 220 Hz beat at exactly the same speed. Weight.

Stratum two

SIGNAL

Eight original wavetables, a phase-modulation pair, a ring modulator and a 64-partial additive bank whose upper partials can walk out of the harmonic series while the fundamental holds. Something has learned to think.

Stratum three

MEMORY

Granular and spectral reading over procedural material, a thirty-second capture or your own imported file, all under one EROSION control that takes it apart four ways at once. The world remembering itself.

Stratum four

STRUCTURE

Seven resonant bodies — plates, cables, beams, cavities, glass, tubes and combs — driven by an impulse, a noise burst, a bow, or any other stratum. STRESS carries them to fracture. Architecture under stress.

It moves by itself

LIFE: eight LFOs, four envelopes, four drawable shapes, four stochastic sources, two followers, four event lanes, and a network that listens.

The LFOs reach from fifty hertz down to one cycle in twenty minutes, free or locked to the host's bars, global or one per voice. The stochastic sources run from a smooth wander through sample-and-hold and cluster bursts to a bounded chaotic trajectory. The event lanes fire with a chance, at a rate, with a refractory wait, at targets like striking the structure or freezing the spectrum.

Above all of that is the LIFE NETWORK: detectors measuring energy, brightness and transients on every stratum and on the mix, offered as modulation sources like any other, with hysteresis and refractory periods so the instrument does not flicker on a threshold. COUPLING is how much one stratum answers another. AUTONOMY is how far the world may drift from what you set. RECOVERY pulls it back.

Everything meets in a 32-slot matrix with signed depth, a second source to scale by, a curve, a slew and a range clamp. Modulation is an offset on what you set — the value you edited never moves underneath you.

One control, thirty minutes

HISTORY: four scenes, and a journey between them.

Set the instrument up the way a piece should start and store it into a scene. Change it and store another. HISTORY then travels between them, interpolating in the domain each parameter belongs to — pitches in semitones, times and frequencies logarithmically, levels in decibels, and anything that is a list or a switch stepping over at the midpoint rather than smearing. The four scenes are called AWAKENING, OCCUPATION, COLLAPSE and AFTERMATH, and you can rename them.

DRIVE is MANUAL — your hand or a host automation lane — or AUTO, running on its own clock over anything from one second to thirty minutes, or AUTO SYNC, running on the host's bars. At the end it stops, loops, or turns round and comes back. HOLD HISTORY freezes the journey where it is so you can sit inside one moment.

It has to be armed, and it needs two scenes. HISTORY is off in a fresh patch and needs at least two different scenes stored. Until both are true, the slider moves and nothing happens — so the panel says so itself, in the line under the scene tiles. Ten of the forty-eight factory patches ship with their scenes already stored and HISTORY already on.

Eight words for what happens next

MASS, DREAD, VIOLENCE, INSTABILITY, CONTAMINATION, DISTANCE, LIFE, HUMANITY.

Eight macros, always visible on every view, each driving up to eight destinations at signed depths. Their meanings are consistent across the bank, so the same word does a recognisable thing on any patch — except HUMANITY, which is patch-specific by design. VIOLENCE is level-matched by the saturators' own measured trackers, so it is abrasion rather than volume. Click a macro's name to see and change what it reaches.

Three ways to play it

DRONE is an explicit switch with a root, a chord, a latch and a spread — so a fresh instance is exactly silent and never starts droning on its own in a busy session. INSTRUMENT is eight voices, poly, mono or legato, with velocity, bend, sustain, aftertouch and MPE. EXCITATION feeds external audio in on the AUX input, either to excite STRUCTURE and fill MEMORY's capture or to duck the instrument under a voice. Drone and keys share the same eight voices, and each stratum has its own switches for which of the two it takes part in.

Four passages

Rendered from the instrument, nothing added. The last one sits under a kick and a bass line, to show what it does with room left.
