Mix

Brown is the deep one — distant surf, the hum of a cabin at altitude — and masks the most for the least hiss. Green sits in the middle of the band, near 500 Hz, where wind in trees lives. Rain is a wash of far-off drops with nearer ones pattering through it; soft rain is the same weather heard through a closed window. Ocean rises and falls on its own swell. Run one on its own, or lay several over each other — brown under soft rain is where most people end up.

MonoStereo3D

Halfway along is plain stereo, which is how the app has always sounded. Left of it the two channels are drawn together towards one, which is worth having for a single earbud. Right of it each sound is built as a handful of sources standing around you instead of as two channels: every one of them reaches the two ears a fraction of a millisecond apart, shaded by the head in between and coloured for its height, with a soft room behind the lot. Rain then falls mostly overhead, soft rain gathers at a window off to the left and the ocean lies a long way out in front. Headphones hear nearly all of it — that is where the noise stops sitting on the phone and starts filling the room; a speaker hears the least, though it loses nothing, since the bottom of the band is folded back to the middle where one speaker was going to put it anyway. Moving the right-hand half rebuilds the loops, which takes a moment; the left-hand half is instant.
