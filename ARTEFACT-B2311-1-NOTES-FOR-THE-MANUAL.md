## The object, in one paragraph

Accession 1 — the lowest number in the field record and catalogued last. It was
collected in the first week from scree above Kell Rille and logged as a nodule,
and it sat in a crate for two years. What made anyone look again was that two
independent timing units stored in the same shelter, which should have drifted
apart at their own rates, did not. The object had been counting the whole time,
and it had been counting *at* them.

That is also the physics, and the coincidence is the point: whatever else this
thing is, it pulls other clocks towards its own.

## What it is

A lattice of nine thousand two hundred and sixteen counters. Each holds a count
that rises at its own rate; on reaching the top it discharges, resets, and shoves
each of its neighbours forward. A shove can carry a neighbour over with it, so
discharges cascade — and the length of a cascade is not designed, it is what the
coupling produces.

**Its sound is not a vibration.** There is no oscillator anywhere in it. The
waveform is the list of moments at which something happened, and rhythm, pitch
and colour are one quantity — the rate of events — heard at three scales.

## The four things worth stating in the report

**It counts faster when heated, and at 77 K it does not count at all.** Not
almost: the loop is skipped and the output is 0.000000. 7226 discharges a second
at 185 K, 13640 at 330, 28826 at 511, 69360 at 800.

**It eases towards an imposed pulse and never arrives.** Gathering onto the beat
rises *across a take* — 0.128 to 0.439 at three pulses a second, 0.280 to 0.684
at eight. It is a degree, not a lock.

**It can be led and cannot be forced.** Gathering peaks at 0.525 for a gentle
pulse and has fallen to 0.158 when the pulse is three times as hard. Once the
lattice has organised itself, a nudge aligns its clusters and a shove scatters
them. The survey has no account of why a thing that appears built to be joined
would refuse to be driven — that is a good open question for the report.

**It holds several timings at once.** Parts near each other in rate run
together, so the body falls into regions rather than one group; six distinct
recurring intervals were logged in a single passage, with 84.9 % of steps still
empty.
